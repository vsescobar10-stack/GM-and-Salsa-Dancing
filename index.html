A Video Letter to My Future Self

Record one short video in which today's you gives advice to your future self at three hard moments of professional life.

  • 3questions
  • ~1 minper question
  • ~3 minin total
Go to the questions

How to record

  1. One video, about 1 minute per question (3 minutes in total).
  2. Speak directly to your future self ("when this happens, remember that..."). Use notes, not a script.
  3. A phone is enough. Find a quiet place, with your face visible.
  4. Keep the video somewhere you can find it. It is meant to be opened when the moment comes.

Fixed vs. growth mindset in a nutshell

A fixed mindset treats ability as a given. A growth mindset treats it as something you develop through effort, good strategies and feedback (Dweck). Hover or tap a line to see its counterpart.

Fixed mindset

Ability
"You either have it or you don't."
Challenges
Avoided: they might expose limits.
Effort
Proof that I'm not talented.
Mistakes
Proof that I'm not good enough.
Feedback
A threat to defend against.
Others' success
A threat; I compare myself.

Growth mindset

Ability
"I can develop it."
Challenges
Sought: they are how I grow.
Effort
The path to mastery.
Mistakes
Information about what to adjust.
Feedback
A tool to improve.
Others' success
A source of ideas and inspiration.

How you experienced it in the seminar

The seminar used salsa as a mirror: it puts you in situations of exposure, error and feedback, where each mindset shows up in the body before you can talk about it. The table follows the first three core sessions of the syllabus. These are the moments to draw on when you answer the three questions below.

SessionSituation in the danceFixed-mindset voiceGrowth-mindset response
1. Self-awareness and attention Learning new steps next to others; noticing where your attention goes. "Everyone is better than me." "I'll stop comparing. What does my own progress look like?"
2. Self-motivation and effort Repeating a step that doesn't work yet; pushing a limit you assumed was fixed. "I'm just not a dancer." "Not yet. Effort and a different strategy can change this."
3. Discomfort and feedback Making mistakes in front of a partner; giving and receiving peer feedback. "They saw me fail." "This feedback tells me what to adjust next."

The three questions

Prepare each answer first, then record.

Question 1

When I'm rejected from my dream job

Question to recordIf I get rejected from the dream job I was applying for, the one I was so excited about, what advice do I give myself so that I don't read it as a verdict on my worth?

Being rejected from a dream job you were really excited about hurts. A fixed mindset turns it into a sentence about who you are. A growth mindset reads it as information about where you are today and what you can develop.

Ideas for your advice (use the ones that feel true to you)

  • What will my harshest inner voice say in that moment, and how do I answer it?
  • What might have been missing that I can develop, and what is my first step?
  • Who can give me honest feedback about the process?
  • What is still true about me, even if I wasn't chosen?

Idea behind it

The difference between "I'm not good enough" and "not yet."

Optional opening line: Hi, future me. If you're watching this, you were rejected from the dream job you were so hopeful about. Before you draw conclusions about yourself, remember that...

Question 2

When I make my first big mistake at work

Question to recordThe day I make my first important mistake at work, what advice do I give myself to respond with responsibility and without sinking into shame?

The first serious mistake almost always comes. A fixed mindset takes it as proof that you aren't capable and pushes you to hide it or defend yourself. A growth mindset takes it as part of learning without making it any less serious: you own your part and extract what to learn.

Ideas for your advice (use the ones that feel true to you)

  • What will my first impulse probably be (hide it, justify it, blame someone), and what will I do instead?
  • Who do I tell first, and how: with facts and a proposed solution?
  • What is the difference between "I made a mistake" and "I am a mistake"?
  • What will I change so it doesn't happen again?

Idea behind it

A mistake is data and deliberate practice, and guilt ("I did something wrong") is not shame ("I am something wrong"), a distinction from Brené Brown. In the seminar, dancing put you in exactly this spot: getting it wrong in front of others and continuing to dance.

Optional opening line: Future me, if you're here, you got something important wrong. Breathe. The first thing I want you to remember is...

Question 3

When I feel unmotivated

Question to recordWhen I've lost my drive and everything costs twice as much, what advice do I give myself to start moving again, even with one small step?

Demotivation doesn't warn you, and it doesn't mean you chose wrong. A growth mindset doesn't ask you to fake enthusiasm: it invites you to reconnect with effort, with the purpose behind what you do, and with small progress you can actually see.

Ideas for your advice (use the ones that feel true to you)

  • How do I recognize I'm at that point, and what signs come before it?
  • What did I achieve in the past that seemed impossible at the start?
  • What is the smallest step I can take today?
  • What am I doing this for? Which of my values is at stake?
  • Who can I call?

Idea behind it

Effort and strategy as the road to progress, the value of recognizing progress (not only results), and Susan David's emotional agility: feeling what you feel without letting it decide for you.

Optional opening line: Hi, future me. If you're watching this, you have no energy for anything today. That's okay. Before you decide what to do about it, look at...
